My rating of this film might be heavily influenced by my expectations of it going in. Gattaca is one of the classic sci-fi films I would hear about, and so I expected it to be quite good. In the end, the premise and idea behind it are quite interesting, but I think the execution lacked. It might well be worthy of an A-, but I'm giving it a B+ because I felt like close inspection of the plot through the lense of any of the themes revealed major cracks.

# My Complaints
The movie did not start off well for me with a very long segment of voice-over narration. It felt a lot like The Shawshank Redemption where the plot that is being discussed is somewhat interesting, but by skipping over so much of it a lot of that interest goes away. Sometimes that tradeoff is worth it for the ability to give a backstory concisely. In this case I think it was an actively bad choice because it weakens our understanding of the relationship between the brothers, and while that initially seems fine, it comes back to be a big part of the story. For me I also got a little confused because the story skips past a real explanation of why Vincent decided to leave his family (at least at that moment in particular), and so it wasn't obvious to me that that was what he was doing.

My real gripe with this movie is what seems to be the climactic quote when Vincent goes for another swim with Anton and beats him again. He explains how he did this by saying that he saved nothing for the swim back. But that just doesn't really make any sense. If that were actually true, Anton should have had to drag him back to the shore himself. Not only did that not happen, but the opposite actually happened! Anton (despite presumably saving something for the swim back), suddenly starts drowning. Vincent (who saved nothing for the way back), now has to swim back with not only his own weight but also his brother's. It might sound like I'm complaining about just a small plot hole, but to me it's more than that. This line is basically the thesis of the movie (or at least it's very much framed that way). To have it not make sense with the plot, especially when the entire purpose of the swim in the first place is just as a metaphor for this thesis, just destroys everything.

# A Little Praise
Ok, but let's say some nice things about this movie too, because it really isn't as bad as this may have made it look. Gattaca is a movie all about the human spirit, and it does a good job of choosing characters who would have interesting perspectives on this topic. Vincent is our main character who is told he has no potential and must prove the world wrong. But equally interesting is Jerome who is told he has every potential and is defeated by his own expectations. Irene is halfway between, being told she is capable of so much, but not quite everything, and who basically lives up to that but feels forever judged for her imperfection. And Anton is the foil to Jerome -- he also does not live up to his potential, but he keeps his superior attitude and believes in the system nonetheless.

I watched this movie as a part of Short Story Symposium, and the discussion led me to another interesting idea. In some sense this film is the antithesis of [Whiplash](whiplash). In Whiplash you watch someone give up everything for their dream, and generally you're lead to believe that this is a mistake. That they have been convinced to tolerate abuse for the sake of the dream and that doing staking all of your self worth in one achievement is unhealthy. Gattaca is almost the exact opposite as we watch Vincent sacrifice everything and generally we are cheering him on and hoping for his success. We hold him and his determination up as an example of the indomitable nature of the human will. The point of this comparison isn't to say that one is right and one is wrong -- they have quite different contexts for sure -- but is rather just to highlight the ways different films can tackle similar issues from opposite sides.

# A Fun theory
I also want to record a theory of mine while watching the film. I was pretty cognizant of Irene as a character throughout. I kept wondering what her purpose was in the film. In the end I feel like it was mostly just as a love interest and as eye candy and that quite annoyed me. But before I knew that was the case, I thought that she might be the real murderer. I understand that the movie wants to say something with the fact that the other director was the murderer despite having good genes, but I thought that perhaps Irene was someone exactly like Vincent as well. I don't know if that would have made a better movie, but it would have certainly made a better character, and it was a fun theory to have as I watched!
