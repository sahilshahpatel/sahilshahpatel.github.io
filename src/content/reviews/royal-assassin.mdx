My general thoughts from [The Assassin's Apprentice] still hold, so I'll focus this on specifics.

Verity and Kettricken come to life in this book. They quickly became my favorite characters (Kettricken especially). Verity didn't feel too real and fleshed out to me in the first, and I think the developments in this book were necessary to make Fitz's loyalty to him justified. I also liked how the Fool talked about him having a different king than Fitz in a way. That added a level of nuance to Fitz's loyalties that I didn't really expect. I will say that Regal feels 2D still. There is a small section that suggests his motivation may be that he believes Shrewd killed his mother, but it wasn't strongly enough tied to the events of the novel to satisfy me. That being said, there is clearly a lot of setup for a payoff that I expect to occur in the final book of the trilogy.

I saw some reviews that disliked the romance with Molly. For my part, I loved it. I tend to be a believer in small happinesses. That ambition and lofty goals concerning the world at scale are less important than the impacts of our local scale relationships. I loved that Hobb explored that through Molly. She showed why my approach has value but also why it makes little sense when the world is in peril. I also found the repetitiveness of Fitz's thoughts about Molly impactful because they served as a demonstration to the reader of his emotional transition from regular boy to a Chade-like tool.

Funny enough, what I found more repetitive through these last two books was his bonds with animals. Twice in the first book was already a lot for me, and Smithy was so obviously a plot device to me that I struggled to get into it. When Nighteyes came around I felt similar -- worried that he was a short term plot device soon to be used. In the end he kind of was, but he wasn't used all the way up, and so it bothered me less. But I definitely didn't care nearly as much for Fitz relationship with Nighteyes as I did with Verity, Kettricken, the Fool, Burrich, Molly, Patience, or even Shrewd.

With that said, I did read this book even faster than the first despite it being longer, so I am not complaining on the whole. This series remains my favorite fantasy I've read as an adult and would likely be my favorite fantasy series ever if I stopped to take account.
