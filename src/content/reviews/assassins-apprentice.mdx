The Assassin's Apprentice is the adult fantasy novel I have been waiting for! As I've grown older I've found myself enjoying realistic fiction more for the way it often centers it's stories on emotional developments in its characters. Most books have some element of that, but most fantasy I've read as an adult has focused more on the action, combat, and scale leaving me wanting for a more personal center. All of that had me hesitant in reading another fantasy series. I had a few people telling me to read Robin Hobb earlier in the year and I ignored them mostly. But by the end too many people unbeknownst to each other had told me to read it, and I decided it was a sign I should try it.

And I'm so glad I did! This book has everything! The core story focused on Fitz and his coming of age as he absorbs the reality of these larger world-scale issues around him is just so perfect. His core conflict of loneliness and feeling ostracized is also a theme that always captures me, and I was amazed at how well that feeling could co-exist with characters like Burrich or Chade who did have warmth for Fitz. Hobb managed to convey that he did have companionship and even the beginnings of community, and yet it didn't do much to console his lonely soul. Burrich, Chade, Shrewd, and Verity are also such good characters. You admire them at times and hate them at others. You never feel they are so perfect or so flawed, but just the right balance to instead be real.

And the world itself! The Wit and the Skill, the politics of the kingdom, the mystery of the Red Ships, all of it and more is so enticing without distracting from the emotional story.

I was warned before reading that there isn't too much of a plot or that the book is slow. I disagree heavily with the first, and the second I feel is subjective. This book was an absolute page-turner for me. I was at a family wedding while reading it and found myself showing up late to events because I was so consumed by it. I could see why you'd say it's slow, but if you are as captured by a character study as I am, then Fitz's everyday life and troubles are not slow at all.

I am writing this review after having already finished the second book, and I cannot wait for more of this series.
