This is a book that feels like a journal entry. The author clearly has a lot of anger to process, and I agree with most of that anger. At the same time, this is supposed to be a narrative story, not an essay. I think the story suffers greatly from a lack of focus. The author throws in one liners and commentary about society in random places unrelated to what's happening. The character (in my view) is not very consistently characterized -- she feels so different at different times. The main plot point of her leaving the island makes no sense -- she doesn't want to do to others what her mom did to her, and yet she doesn't even tell anyone anything. She doesn't talk to even Angie?? It's all just weird and doesn't seem to follow a consistent idea or theme throughout besides just anger at the way the world is.

For readers who are similarly angry, it could be super cathartic. I could see people liking it. But it wasn't for me.
