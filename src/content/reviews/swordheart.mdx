Swordheart is a perfectly enjoyable, if juvenile, romantasy. As compared to ACOTAR, I liked that the book didn't try to be some grand fantasy or political intrigue novel. It sets up a super interesting world, but it stays small stakes which is correct for the tone.

To me [main character] was a little annoying. The book is trying to show us that she just needs to find her kind of people, but personally it fell into the trap of the "quirky girl" main character who doesn't feel real to me. [Sword man] was also a little over the top, but since he was plausibly a genie I didn't mind as much.

The pacing was also a little slow with how much they just kept going back and forth on the same road, but I took that mostly as a joke. Speaking of which, this book was pretty funny. There were a lot of jokes which got old or felt juvenile to me, but even so there were laugh at loud moments too.

The biggest credit I'll give this book is its world building actually. It's not a focus of the novel (as it shouldn't be), but it's done in perfectly small amounts, and the world is so interesting for it.

Recommending this book is a little hard for me. It feels juvenile in a way that would have me recommending it to middle school or early high school students. But it is also definitely an adult romance. That being said, many of my adult friends thoroughly enjoyed it, so perhaps that's just me.
