Coming into the release of Avatar: Seven Havens I had mixed feelings. The recent Netflix live-action Avatar adaptation was super disappointing (even with low expectations to begin with). On the other hand, the animated movie [Avatar Aang the Last Airbender](avatar-aang-the-last-airbender) was actually pretty decent. The trailer for Seven Havens came out at the same time as that movie, and when I watched it I was disappointed by the animation style a bit, and confused about the story. All in all, the movie gave me enough hope that I decided to watch the show, but the show's trailer didn't give me enough to expect much.

The show released in four sets of episodes (three sets of three and one set of four). The first three were a pretty good start, getting me interested in the central mysteries this new version of the world had to offer and introducing me to the characters I should care about. The second three felt lack-luster to me at the time, probably because the Faceless did not feel super relevant to the larger questions and I didn't understand their purpose. The scary diamond and its related illness were also used quite obtusely as plot devices in a way I didn't find too interesting. The third three I quite liked again, probably because they just started expanding the world and getting back to those bigger mysteries. They also tied in the Faceless in a way that retroactively made them more interesting. The final four episodes were more mixed. 10 and 11 felt really fast in a way that was disappointing. The ending of the finale, though, brought up new and interesting questions that made me excited about the future of the show.

In a second I'll dive in to some more specific thoughts, but I want to draw a comparison to The Dragon Prince for a second. That show was supposed to be a spiritual successor to Avatar, and it also had this idea of 7 types of magic that Callum would have to spiritually acclimate to. In that show I found the pacing of the first two seasons great, and the final moment of season two reminds me a lot of the end of this season of Seven Havens. It finally crystallized the connection between its magic system and its philosophy, and the moment of that felt really well done. However, my complaints about that show (which I stopped watching I think partway through season four?) are all about pacing. It felt like they spent a lot of time building up to that moment, and then once it came they tried to just shove a ton of stuff in in ways that felt less emotional and intentional then before. Seven Havens is actually overall worse to me where the pacing and intentionality feels lack-luster even in the beginning. But that final moment still hits just as much as The Dragon Prince Season 2's did. And the themes, while disappointingly executed, still existed on at least the level of the Avatar Aang movie, and so there is plenty of room for them to improve over time.

So anyways, let's get into specifics.

# Pacing
The show's pacing of character moments felt consistently too fast. I always describe this as feeling like I'm watching an outline rather than a show; the story beats are all there and make sense, but the small stuff in between where they actually __earn__ those moments is lacking. Pavi and Nisha are both childish, but that I got accustomed to given that they are in fact little children. Still, though, Avatar generally knows how to have mature characters talk to immature ones. And it usually knows how to handle middle-matureness too. In that respect, Karthik did not live up to Iroh or even Tenzin. And Jae was not as good as a Sokka or a Zuko either. I think the show overall needed a less complex plot in order to give it the breathing time required for the emotional checkpoints it had set out for itself.

(After finishing the finale, I had a random thought that I wonder if you could re-write this season without the Faceless at all and save a bunch of time in doing so without sacrificing any of the emotional beats. I might eventually try to test out this theory, but that's just another project on the to-do pile.)

There were also smaller-scale issues like when Kayoko is able to turn the meeting of the envoys from chaos to full agreement in the 30 or so seconds that we follow Jae into the kitchens. Karthik has a few moments of just switching opinions super quickly too. And there was an insane over-use of the "crowd all boos a thing until leader says one word and then crowd all cheers". In those crowd scenes it felt like they were trying to illustrate leaders losing control of the situations, but then the way they made their recover made it instead seem like the crowd was full of idiots and followers who don't actually hold their own opinion outside of what is convenient to the plot. All of those instances are also places where more time needs to be given towards illustrating what happened and how a character changed their own mind or the minds of others.

# Animation
The animation in this show is inconsistent and odd. Some of it is pretty and some of it feels really off. In particular I found episodes 10 and 11 to be jarring in their transitions and camera movements a few times. There were some similar moments earlier in the show but I don't recall them as well. From what I've heard they outsourced the animation to multiple studios, and I think there was an obvious shift in quality episode to episode and even scene to scene sometimes.

# Fight Scenes and Bending
I'm willing to accept that the post-Cataclysm world is not as good at bending as Aang's or Korra's. So it doesn't bother me that the bending is simple. But the fight scenes and bending in this show were just poorly choreographed. In Avatar, the element usually bends smoothly with movement or effort. Seven Havens, though, had a majority of moments that felt more akin to Shyamalan's movie where movements are made and then an element just goes flying. There were also a couple of moments where they seemed to intend for a character to make a nice "parry" move, but instead they actually sort of pre-fired. I recall a specific moment in the last four episodes when Agam shoots ice at her feet randomly and then like a second later some projectile lands there. That kind of stuff just makes the whole thing feel stilted and wrong, and was disappointing to see.

# The Large-Scale Theme
I don't know where exactly this show is going, but it seems that while Aang had to learn all four types of bending and learn the philosophies of those, we're going to see Pavi (and probably Nisha) instead unlock all seven chakras and learn their philosophies. I'm actually super down for that as a concept as someone who thought The Guru was way too fast for such a significant character moment. I am worried that seven is just too high a number, and that it will end up feeling repetitive and like video game levels to be accomplished. That could easily rob the show of what is an amazing thematic premise.

# Predictions
I thought for a long time that the chakras Pavi saw were the world's chakras. The finale pretty much confirms that they were just Pavi's chakras. However, it's clear that the world's seven havens each correspond to a chakra, and so I wonder if there is still some connection. If there is, I don't know how Nisha fits into that. In any case, I'll go out on a limb and try to be specific with this prediction (and ensure that I look silly when it turns out to be wrong).

This season brought up a few mysteries. The question of who their parents were ended up being the central one that was "solved" in this season. But that still has open questions like how they contacted the White Lotus and what the initial plan for that was. The main mystery right now is how did the world become this way? And that centers on Korra and the Cataclysm. There is also the mystery of what happened to Republic Forest. Likely that is related to the Cataclysm too.

My prediction is that questions about the White Lotus will be the shorter-term focus of the story. Pavi is going out to other havens and is going to meet some White Lotus folks. There is also an open thread of Karthik being kind of evil himself (e.g. his "tough decisions" lines and the whole "lying to society for their own good" philosophy). I could see a world where these other White Lotus members do not cooperate with Karthik but are willing to help train Pavi. As it stands, Karthik had outsized influence on Nisha, but other members could have their own influence on Pavi. (It's also possible evil Karthik was just a red herring, but I'd be pretty disappointed in that.)

The longer-term focus will be on the Cataclysm. I think halfway through the show we understand what happened, and the second half will be about fixing it. That being said, if there are only four seasons, then I think we won't find out until season three, and season four will be about fixing it. The finding out part will likely take place in Republic Forest, and I could see this being where Pavi and Nisha meet back up (though it wouldn't shock me if that happens in season two).

I also think we'll see Pavi/Nisha unlock multiple chakras at once, at least eventually. There are too many to go and not enough time, plus I'd love to see a nuanced story that touches on two philosophical blockages at the same time!
