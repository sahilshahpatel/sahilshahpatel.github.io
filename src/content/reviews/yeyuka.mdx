My first reading of this story didn't impress me particularly, but upon discussion I found it endlessly fascinating. Egan has constructed a story that investigates privilege, morality, and purpose so insightfuly.

Martin shows obvious privilege but is also self aware, which allows for both positive and negative analysis. Similarly we are sympathetic to the local characters, but their blunt and drastic requests do cross lines. The story takes on a different character when taken more literally as an exploration of self-sacrifice for the good of a society vs. self-sacrifice for the good of other specific individuals -- the first feels good and honorable, and yet in everyday life we caution people against being people pleasers and praise assertiveness. All of these dual perspectives (and more) made this story stand out in my mind and be worthy of individual review.
