I read this book because I wanted to see how the original played out before seeing the movie adaptation. I already knew the movies had changed enough that the they couldn't follow to closely, but still I had to see.

Ultimately, I was disappointed. Dune Messiah follow's in Dune's footsteps of saying that Paul is trying to thread the needle of destiny without making me feel that way. (Perhaps this is not an apt comparison, but the writing of the Fool in [Assassin's Apprentice](assassins-apprentice) is a better version of this I think).

This book is an expansion of the political world of Dune, and so if you were looking for that, perhaps you would be pleased. To me though it felt like the characters talked for more than half the book about what they thought would happen, and then we watched as it sort of did but sort of didn't.

There were also the classic older literature issues I expected to have: Chani continues to be a nothing character, and Irulan sort of follows in her footsteps. Alia is not as fleshed out as I would have liked, and her match with Duncan is as contrived as the lampshade that was hung on it.

It's not to say that the ideas of the story weren't somewhat interesting. But Dune had that plus some of the coming of age aspects of Paul's assent. Without that element this time, I found the book much less satisfying.
