This movie was not made to be enjoyed but to be discussed. In that respect I find myself comparing it to [The Drama](the-drama) a lot. The fact that this was even made is astounding, and they were really only able to make it into a story by being willing to be as much the observed as the observer.

What makes this film so interesting to me is that there are four perspectives on a situation, all of which give us glimpses of the truth through their contrast. Elizabeth and Nathan you expect to be in this list, but Billy and especially Amanda are what made it transcend my expectations.

I have two major thoughts I want to write down. The first is that I loved how Amanda and Nathan enhanced the story of the other. In my view, Amanda does not come off super well I'm this film. Not that I judge her too harshly, but she absolutely gets defensive when challenged on The Dropout, and her response left me feeling like she needed to convince herself that that show was ethical more than she needed to convince us. But her presence also allows for some doubt to be sown about Nathan's feelings too. I personally felt uncertain about Nathan throughout the film, and the choice to end it as they did with their conversation to me highlights that they wanted that uncertainty to be visible. This I find fascinating because it is so vulnerable. For the documentarian to reveal to their audience that they are not so sure of what they film. It's almost the opposite of Amanda's reaction (granted, they had time and distance to consider it which Amanda did not), which adds to its intrigue.

The second major thought I had is about why Elizabeth was ok with what she learned in the prison visit. My theory is that when two narcissists like that fall in love, they finally find someone who they believe might be on their level. And so the other person claiming to know exactly what they would do must be fit into their self deception the same as any other inconsistency. And so Billy claims to know what Elizabeth would do and do it for her, and Elizabeth finds this reassuring rather than a betrayal.

There is so much that this movie leaves you to consider. About manipulation, self-deception, grooming, confrontation, self-awareness, and more. It's hard to encapsulate here, but for what it was trying to do, it is one of the best, and for that it gets an S-.
